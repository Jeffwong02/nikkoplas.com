Service Overview

What Is Ultrasonic Welding?

Ultrasonic welding uses high-frequency mechanical vibration (20–40 kHz) to generate localised heat at the joint interface between two thermoplastic parts. The plastic melts and fuses under controlled pressure, then solidifies into a permanent, hermetic bond. The whole weld cycle typically takes less than one second — making it ideal for high-volume assembly.

No Adhesives or Fasteners

The bond is formed by the plastic itself — eliminating adhesive cost, cure time, and the risk of chemical incompatibility with the substrate.

Hermetic Seal Capable

Properly designed weld joints can achieve air- and liquid-tight seals — required for enclosures exposed to moisture, dust (IP-rated housings), or fluid containment.

Fast Cycle Times

Weld cycles under one second support high-volume production schedules — the process integrates directly into the moulding line without slowing throughput.

ISO-Certified Quality

Weld strength, flash inspection, and dimensional checks are conducted under our ISO 9001:2015 quality system with full batch traceability.

Typical Use Cases
